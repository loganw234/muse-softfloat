## 1. Executive summary

**The arithmetic library is in very good shape.** Every independent
check against x86-64 hardware, glibc and MPFR came back clean:

| Check | Volume |
|---|---|
| Randomized and structured differential (lead) | about 388 million checks |
| Review agents' own differentials | roughly another 1.1 billion (core, character-conversion and Tier 2 agents) |
| Exhaustive binary16 add and mul, ties-to-even | 2 × 2³² operand pairs |
| Berkeley TestFloat 3e cases (an agent's run) | 8.4 million |
| Mutation probes of the source (two agents' runs) | 21 + 199 mutants; the survivors were mostly equivalent, plus three genuine blind-spot classes (L8) |

None of it found a wrong result or a wrong flag. The project's own gates
pass on Linux (`standard`, and `sanitize` with full ASan+UBSan). A
32-bit ILP32 build passes all 411,213 committed vectors. The library
compiles without a single warning under gcc and clang with `-Wpedantic`
(clang also with `-Wconversion` and `-Wsign-conversion`). Every
quantitative README claim that the tree can support recounts
correctly.

**The weaknesses are in the verification story around the library, not
in the library.** The project's thesis is "the gate is the product".
The committed gate is narrower than the claims made for it:

- **The executable semantic authority never runs in the tree.** Berkeley
  TestFloat is named for 117 instantiations, and 690 coverage rows cite
  `testfloat-*` vector sets that do not exist in the tree.
- **The gate cannot notice its own vectors being deleted.** Removing
  about 82,000 committed vectors still produces `VERDICT: PASS`.
- **Some "permanent negative controls" cannot fail for the reason their
  names give.**
- **CI's sanitize job can go green without running a sanitizer.**
- **The Tier 2 vectors miss gross regressions in parts of the domain.**
  A one-token mutation that corrupts binary64 exp2m1 over most of
  [165, 1025) still passes every committed vector.

**Around the library**, the findings are robustness and documentation
issues in the board firmware, host tools, compat layer, site and demo
scripts. There are also repository-hygiene items: local Windows paths
inside the published WebAssembly, about 270 MiB of history that is 96% video,
and GPL fragments in an MIT-labelled script.

There are **no Critical findings**. Two findings are rated **High**.
Both are about the verification claims, both are cheap to fix, and the
independent re-runs above confirm that neither currently hides a defect.

**Library-level findings are all Low.** They are:

- a NaN-sign detail when a signaling NaN is narrowed (L1);
- rounding-attribute values that are never validated (L2);
- the documented beyond-20-digit decimal-input route and its
  consequences (L3);
- a silent wrong answer for decimal or hex inputs longer than about
  100,000 characters (L4);
- an undocumented worst case of about 3.1 KB of stack (L5);
- a missing `extern "C"` guard (L6);
- a wrong ABI comment in the header (L7).

None changes a result for ordinary inputs. L4 is the only wrong-value
defect found anywhere in the library, and it needs pathological input.

### 2.3 Independent oracle (lead) [V]

The oracle is written for this audit and shares no code with the
project. Its sources are in [`oracle/`](oracle/) and its runner is
`bash docs/audit/oracle/run.sh`.

**The reference is x86-64 hardware**, used only where it is
unambiguous:

- SSE2/FMA3 scalar operations under each of the four MXCSR rounding
  modes, with flags read from MXCSR. x86 detects tininess after
  rounding, the same choice as SF-0 §2.5.
- F16C `vcvtps2ph` for binary16.
- AVX-512F unsigned conversions.
- SSE4.1 `roundsd`.

**For results narrower than binary64**, the operation is computed in
binary64 with round-to-odd (RTZ plus a sticky bit in the LSB). It is
then rounded once to the destination, so there is no double rounding.

**roundTiesToAway** is derived exactly. A result is an exact tie only
when the round-to-odd intermediate equals the midpoint of the
destination's RTZ and away neighbours. At a tie, the away rounding is
taken from hardware; otherwise the RNE result is used. For binary64
FMA and tiny products and quotients, the exact value comes from MPFR
at 3,300 bits.

**For the remaining families**, glibc 2.39's C23 functions are the
reference: `fminimum*`/`fmaximum*`, `totalorder*`, `getpayload`,
`setpayload*`, `nextup`/`nextdown`, `logb`, `scalbn`, `remainder`.
Comparisons and classification use direct rule tables.

**NaN results** are checked two ways. First for NaN-ness and flags
against IEEE rules. Then, for payloads, against the project's own
SF-0 §2.5 matrix.

| Oracle run | Coverage | Checks | Mismatches |
|---|---|---|---|
| `hwdiff` (25× scale) | add/sub/mul/div for all 27 dest×src format combinations; sqrt and convertFormat for all 9; FMA for all 81 dest×src combinations; 24 convertFromInt; all 240 convertToInteger forms; all 18 roundToIntegral forms. All five rounding attributes; binary16 unary sources exhaustive | 248,140,440 | **0** |
| | of which: NaN-operand cases (flags and NaN-ness) | 17,227,006 | 0 |
| `hwdiff2` (10× scale) | all 198 comparison predicates incl. mixed formats; the 24 Clause 9.6 min/max operations; totalOrder/Mag; payload ops; remainder; nextUp/Down; logB; scaleB (5 modes); class, predicates and sign ops (binary16 exhaustive) | 130,259,840 | **0** (setPayload(−0) differences excluded; see I1) |
| `mpta` | binary64 ties-to-away for FMA, tiny products and subnormal quotients (the cases `hwdiff` skips) | 1,189,965 (117,447 exact ties) | **0** |
| `mpt2` (MPFR 4.2, emulated formats with subnormals) | Tier 2: sinPi, cosPi, exp2, exp2m1, log2, log2p1, rSqrt, hypot; binary32 and binary64; te/tz/tp/tn; finite non-special inputs biased to hard regions (near integers and half-integers, near 1, tiny, overflow/underflow edges) | 8,401,260 | **0** |
| `f16exh` | binary16 add and mul, every ordered operand pair, ties-to-even | 2 × 4,294,967,296 | **0** |

Two negative controls show the oracle can fail:

- A library built with `-DMSF_TININESS_AFTER_ROUNDING=0` gives **597
  mismatches** (as reproduced by `run.sh`), all at the smallest-normal
  boundary.
- Calling the library with ties-to-even where the oracle expects
  ties-to-away gives **58,883 mismatches**, about half of the
  constructed ties.

Three oracle bugs were found and fixed while bringing it up; none was a
library issue:

- the sign of exact-zero sums in the round-to-odd path;
- the binary64 integer range limit 2⁶³ being treated as representable;
- `exp2` results that underflow even MPFR's widest exponent range
  being treated as "not tiny". The library correctly raises
  underflow+inexact there.

The f16 exponent generator also had a cosmetic bug, now fixed.

## 4. Findings

Severity scale:

- **High:** a headline claim is materially overstated, or the gate can
  give a false PASS in a supported workflow.
- **Medium:** a real weakness with limited or latent impact.
- **Low:** robustness, consistency or clarity.
- **Info:** an observation, a judgment call, or something worth knowing.

### 4.1 High

#### H1. The Berkeley TestFloat authority is not exercised by anything committed or run in the tree [V]

**Where.** README.md:140-142 ("Each row is gated against Berkeley
TestFloat 3e, MPFR, or an exact reference…"); `docs/COVERAGE.tsv`
(`authority` and `vectors` columns); `tools/run.sh` (every budget);
`tests/berkeley_ingest.py:90-98`; `tests/VECTORS.md:82,125-126`;
`docs/metrics/fetches.jsonl` (TestFloat row: "vectors stream through
testfloat_gen at gate time").

**What the ledger says.** COVERAGE.tsv names `berkeley` as the deciding
authority for **117** instantiations. These are all homogeneous
add/sub/mul/div/sqrt/fma, roundToIntegral, remainder, convertFormat,
convertFromInt and convertToInteger rows. Another **111** rows say
`project-exact+berkeley-check`, and **690 of 849** rows list a
`testfloat-l2` vector set.

**What the tree contains.**

- Of the 411,213 committed vectors, **0** carry a `berkeley:` tag. All
  77 tags are `exact-py*`, `exact-f64`, `exact-str`, `mpfr640-trig`,
  `mpfr-alg`, `mpfr-explog` or `exact-py-t2`.
- No budget, no CI job and no runner path invokes `berkeley_ingest.py`
  or `testfloat_gen`. The ingest tool expects an out-of-tree oracle
  build under `../scratch/oracles/`.
- `tests/VECTORS.md` says the runner inventory prints the oracle hash
  and flag translation. It prints neither.
- SF-0 §5.1 requires oracle versions in the inventory.

The Berkeley comparisons really happened. RECORD.md:336-347 and
:408-416 describe more than 37 M post-fix cases, run in the owner's
scratch tree. They are recorded history, not a gate, and they cannot be
reproduced from a clone.

**Why it matters.** RECORD.md:336-341 itself records that a tininess
defect passed every self-consistent in-tree gate. The Python reference
"implemented the same wrong reading", and only Berkeley disagreed. The
in-tree gate alone therefore has a demonstrated blind spot of exactly
the kind the missing authority closes.

**Mitigation found in this audit.** Every one of the 117
Berkeley-authority rows is covered by the hardware differential (§2.3,
0 mismatches). An agent also re-ran Berkeley TestFloat 3e through the
repository's own ingest tool: 8,368,464 cases, 0 mismatches. **No
defect is hidden today.**

**Recommendation.**

- Add an opt-in `oracle` budget plus a CI job that does four things:
  1. fetches SoftFloat/TestFloat by the pinned SHA-256 (as
     `demo/fetch.py` already does for REBOUND/KissFFT);
  2. builds the 8086-SSE specialization;
  3. streams `berkeley_ingest.py` at level 1 with a fixed seed;
  4. verifies, and prints the oracle hash in the inventory.
- Fix M7's `--take` deadlock first.
- Alternatively, or in addition, have `tools/gen_tables.py` split
  COVERAGE's `vectors` column into "gated in-tree" and "one-time
  evidence". Then the ledger cannot cite sets that no budget runs.

#### H2. The gate cannot detect deletion or weakening of its own vector set [A, mechanism verified by the lead]

**Where.** `tools/run.sh:290-292` (also :401-402):
`write_manifest` and `write_expect_counts` hash and count the current
working tree. The verify step runs immediately after, against those
same files. Also `tests/runner.c:689-691,862-878`.

**What.**

- The manifest and the expected per-provenance counts are produced from
  the files under test, in the same run. They therefore always agree
  with those files.
- No committed baseline or count floor exists. The only fixed numbers
  in the gate are the 783/66 instantiation totals in `inventory_check`.
- The agent cut `arith-f64.vec` to one vector and emptied
  `explog-f64.vec` and `aug-f32.vec`. It then regenerated the
  manifest and counts as `run.sh` does. Result: `VERDICT: PASS`,
  total 328,663 instead of 411,213.
- The manifest also omits six compiled-in headers
  (`msf_internal.h`, `msf_decode.h`, `msf_performat.inc`,
  `msf_trig_tables.h`, `msf_explog_tables.h`, `sha256.h`).
- An empty manifest prints "all entries match". An empty or garbage
  expect-counts file is silently ignored.
- `--manifest` or `--expect-counts` with no value is ignored.

**Why it matters.**

- AGENTS.md says "a green run that cannot name its vectors has
  failed". This run names its vectors, but nothing compares them with
  what should be there.
- Two of the seven planted defects that verifiers missed
  (`docs/metrics/plants.jsonl`: w1f32 #5 "boundary quietly weakened
  in generator pool", w5alg1 #3) are this class.
- The drift check covers only the four `gen_tables.py` outputs. The 51
  `.vec` files, the two generated `src/msf_*_tables.h` headers and the
  interval catalogue are not regenerated by any budget, although the
  agent showed that all of them do reproduce.

**Recommendation.**

- Commit a vectors baseline, for example `tests/vectors/EXPECT.tsv`
  holding per-file SHA-256 and per-tag counts. Have the gate refuse
  any difference unless the same commit updates the baseline.
- Make missing or empty `--manifest`/`--expect-counts` arguments fatal.
- Optionally add the cheap generators (`gen_vectors*.py directed`,
  under 30 s total) to `standard` as a byte-for-byte drift check.

### 4.2 Medium

#### M1. CI's `sanitize` job can go green with no sanitizer run [V]

**Where.** `tools/run.sh:351-355,453-455`; `.github/workflows/ci.yml:123-124`.

**What.**

- If the clean canary fails to link with a message naming
  asan or ubsan, the budget prints SKIP-BY-NAME and **exits 0**.
- The CI step runs the budget and checks only the exit status.
- `ci.yml:10-14` and README say a green sanitize job proves the
  instrumentation was live. That is true only while the runner image
  ships the sanitizer runtimes; it does today, and this audit's full
  run found them working.

**Recommendation.**

- In CI, make SKIP-BY-NAME fatal (`MSF_SANITIZE_REQUIRED=1` → exit 1).
  Alternatively, grep the log for `budget sanitize: PASS`.

#### M2. Several permanent negative controls are weaker than their names [V for count-mismatch, x87, and the messages; A for the rest]

**Where.** `tools/run.sh:211-280`.

| Control | Weakness |
|---|---|
| `count-mismatch` (:251-253) | **Passes without its mutation.** It verifies only `encodings-f16.vec` against an expect file that describes all 51 files. The unmodified `expect.txt` already gives `rc=1` and 77 `COUNT MISMATCH` lines (reproduced). The +1 mutation is never what gets refused |
| `x87-compiler-refusal` (:259-263) | SKIP-BY-NAME on every Linux run, including all of CI. It is checked only on the owner's host |
| `stale-manifest` (:247) | `sed '1s/^./0/'` is a no-op whenever the first manifest hash (of `src/msf16.c`) already starts with `0`. That is a 1-in-16 chance per edit that the control spuriously reports "NOT REFUSED" and fails the gate |
| `wrong-nan-expectation` (:226-229) | Flips one class id in `msf*_class` lines. It does not test NaN payload or sNaN→invalid, which is what SF-0 §5.2 names. It checks the exit code only, and it was a silent no-op under CRLF until the CI fix |
| `wrong-rounding-mode`, `wrong-flag-bit`, `wrong-tininess-polarity` | Exercise only the pack kernel or `pack64.vec`, and check exit codes only. Under the tininess mutant, only 17 operation names fail anywhere (see M3) |
| `tier-inventory` (:269) | Prints "refused as required" when the inventory check **passes**. The wording is misleading; this is a positive check, not a refusal |
| summary (:279) | Prints "negative controls: all refused" even when a control was SKIP-BY-NAME |
| in-source Tier 2 controls | Never run by any script [V]. These are `MSF_ALG_TEST_*`, `MSF_AUG_TEST_*`, `MSF_TRIG_DROP_LIMB` (labelled "Permanent negative control only" at `src/msf_math.c:411`), `MSF_EXPLOG_DROP_LIMB`, `*_FORCE_START` and `MSF_EXPLOG_EK_NARROW`. Replayed against the committed Tier 2 vectors: `MSF_TRIG_DROP_LIMB` is still refused (198 failures) and `MSF_ALG_TEST_TRUNC` too (2,318), but **`MSF_EXPLOG_DROP_LIMB` gives 0 failures**. That control has silently stopped refusing |
| controls promised but absent | At P0, RECORD.md:254-260 says the remaining SF-0 §5.2 controls would "arm with their surfaces" in later waves. Four have no watched-failing control in any committed budget script (grep of `tools/run.sh` and `board/*.sh`) [V]: the RP2040 bootrom denormal-flush and NaN-as-infinity cases, a wrong-board-identity run, the decimal-oracle run with a too-narrow exponent range, and the wrapper-state leak (IRQ / second core). `board/common.sh` has a serial-selection guard, but nothing is watched failing |

**Recommendation.**

- `count-mismatch`: run all files, so that the mutated count is the
  only difference.
- `stale-manifest`: corrupt a byte so the line is guaranteed to change
  (for example, flip the hex digit).
- `wrong-nan-expectation`: add a payload mutation and an sNaN-flag
  mutation.
- Print the number of skipped controls in the summary line.
- On Linux, add a refusal control that uses `-m32 -mfpmath=387`
  (FLT_EVAL_METHOD=2). The test-infrastructure agent confirmed, by stubbing `dumpmachine`,
  that `check_host_compiler` refuses this.

#### M3. Tininess-boundary vector coverage is thin [A*; library behaviour verified correct by the lead]

**What.**

- Under the `-DMSF_TININESS_AFTER_ROUNDING=0` build, only **17
  operation names** fail anywhere in the 51 committed files. 14 are
  public; 3 are pack kernels.
- No homogeneous binary32/binary16 add/mul/div vector, and no
  binary64 add/div vector, discriminates tininess polarity.
- The narrowing conversions (`msf16_from_f32`, `msf16_from_f64`,
  `msf32_from_f64`) have **0** smallest-normal boundary vectors. SF-0
  §2.5 requires "targeted smallest-normal-boundary cases per format,
  mode, and conversion direction".
- Tier 2 families, xf arithmetic, and f16 fma/sqrt are all blind to the
  flip.
- A hand-made vector
  `msf32_from_f64 te 380ffffff8000000 -> 00800000 10` passes the real
  build and fails the mutant.

**The library is correct here.** The lead's oracle covers these
boundaries and found 0 mismatches. Its own mutant control fails 597
times at exactly these boundaries. The gap is in the committed
vectors.

**Recommendation.**

- Add per-direction boundary vectors for every narrowing conversion
  and for each arithmetic family, in all five modes.
- Extend the wrong-tininess-polarity control to require failures in
  several named families, not just any failure.

#### M4. The compat layer covers binary64 only, and its "named traps" are partly unwired [A; the trap wiring re-derived by the lead (V)]

**Where.** `compat/msf_aeabi.h:5-24`, `compat/msf_aeabi.c:74-100,227-264`,
`board/CMakeLists.txt:72-86`; SF-0 §3.4.

**What.**

- **No binary32 helpers.** No `__aeabi_fadd`/`fsub`/`fmul`/`fdiv`/
  `fcmp*`/`f2iz`/`i2f`/`ul2f` helpers exist; only `f2d`/`d2f`.
  GCC 13.2 and clang 18 at `-mcpu=cortex-m0plus` emit 18 such helpers
  for ordinary `float` code. With the project's 47-symbol `--wrap`
  list, the real libgcc bodies link in unwrapped.
  - SF-0 §3.4 plans "`__aeabi_fadd` and family". The header (correctly)
    says double only. The README's library description could lead a
    user to expect `float` code to run on this library.
- **Unwired traps.** Twenty "named trap" wrappers are defined but
  absent from the `--wrap` list, so in a linked image the real newlib
  bodies run. They include `ceil`, `trunc`, `round`, `rint`,
  `nearbyint`, `fmax`, `fmin`, `hypot`, `log2`, `exp2`, `remainder`,
  `expm1` and `log1p`. That contradicts `msf_aeabi.h:24`
  ("Everything else libm-shaped is a named trap").
- **Quiet relational helpers.** `__aeabi_dcmplt`/`le`/`gt`/`ge` use
  quiet predicates, so a quiet NaN raises no invalid. RTABI permits
  this for minimal implementations, but it is not Annex F behaviour.
- **IRQ race.** Flag raising is a non-atomic read-modify-write
  (`ldr`/`orrs`/`str`). The per-core state is documented as shared
  between thread and IRQ, but lost updates are not mentioned.

**Recommendation.**

- State plainly in the README and compat header that `float` code is
  not routed through the library. Or add the binary32 helper family
  and the `cfcmp*` assembly shims.
- Wire the 20 traps or delete them.
- Document the IRQ lost-update window, or raise flags with interrupts
  masked.

### 4.3 Low

#### L1. A signaling NaN that narrows to an empty payload loses its sign [V]

**Where.** `src/msf_decode.h:80-96` (`msf_nan_to_dest`); SF-0 §2.5.

**What.** When narrowing shifts away every payload bit of a signaling
NaN, the library returns the **positive** default quiet NaN. A quiet
NaN with the same surviving payload keeps its sign.

| Input | Library | x86 hardware | Berkeley 8086-SSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| `msf16_from_f32(0xff800001)` (−sNaN, payload 1) | `7e00` (invalid raised) | `fe00` | `fe00` (per its commonNaN conversion source; not executed) |
| `msf16_from_f32(0xffc00001)` (−qNaN, payload 1) | `fe00` | `fe00` | `fe00` |
| `msf32_from_f64(0xfff0000000000001)` | `7fc00000` | `ffc00000` | `ffc00000` |

The same applies to every formatOf operation that narrows such an
operand. The oracle counted 174,500 cases at 25× scale.

**Impact.** This is conformant: IEEE 754 leaves the sign of a NaN
result unspecified outside the sign-bit operations, and payload
propagation is a "should". But SF-0 §2.5(b) promises that a signaling
NaN is delivered "quieted, preserving sign". It also restricts the
default-NaN substitution to quiet payloads that would truncate to the
infinity pattern. The implementation comment (`msf_decode.h:17-20`)
states the broader rule the code follows.

**Recommendation.** Keep the operand's sign (the quiet bit alone
already prevents the infinity pattern). Or amend SF-0 §2.5 to the
implemented rule.

#### L2. Out-of-range rounding values are accepted, and one selects an internal mode [V]

**Where.** `src/msf_internal.h:18-27`; every function that takes
`msf_rounding_t`.

**What.**

- `(msf_rounding_t)5` selects the internal roundTiesTowardZero mode:
  `msf32_add(0x3f800000, 0x34400000, 5)` returns the tie-to-zero result
  `3f800001`, while `te` gives `3f800002`. The "Pin P5" comment says
  "no caller-passable `msf_rounding_t` can ever select it".
- The same value 5 acts as plain toward-zero in
  `round_to_integral_exact` and in the decimal digit-rounding step
  [A], so it is not even consistent across the API.
- Values 6, 7, 100 and −1 silently behave as toward-zero.
- UBSan reports nothing, and no flag is raised.

**Recommendation.**

- Map the public enum through a validating table so that RTTZ cannot
  be reached. For invalid values, either define a behaviour (for
  example, signal invalid and return the default NaN) or document that
  they are undefined.
- Add one vector per format to the gate.

#### L3. Inputs longer than 20 significant digits use a documented two-step route with sharp consequences [V]

**Where.** `src/msf_string.c:563-576` (step 1) and `:600-602`, `:639-641`
(flags); `include/muse_softfloat.h:33-34`; SF-0 §2.6; ledger ruling R4
(`audit-bundle/ledger/lead.md:267`).

**What the code does.** With more than H = 20 significant digits, it
first rounds the digit string to 20 digits under the active attribute,
then converts exactly. INEXACT is raised whenever step 1 dropped a
non-zero digit. This matches the header, which promises correct
rounding only for 1..H digits.

**What follows** (reproduced by the lead):

| Input | Result | Exact rounding |
|---|---|---|
| `"0.1000000000000000055511151231257827021181583404541015625"`, the exact value of binary64 `0x3fb999999999999a` | `te` and `ta`: right bits, but **inexact** raised. `tz` and `tn`: `…9999`; `tp`: `…999b`. All with inexact | `…999a` with **no** flags in every mode |
| `"1.79769313486231580793729e308"`, above the RNE overflow threshold `2^1024−2^970` and below `2^1024` | `te` and `ta`: **max-finite with inexact only**. `tz`, `tn` and `tp` agree with exact rounding | `te` and `ta`: +inf with overflow+inexact. `tz` and `tn`: max-finite with inexact. `tp`: +inf with overflow+inexact |
| `"1.797693134862315907734e308"`, just above `2^1024` | `tz`: max-finite with inexact only, so the **overflow flag is missing** | `tz`: max-finite with overflow+inexact |

The agent measured, for 21-40-digit strings:

- 40 te mismatches against glibc in 723,548 random cases;
- about 47% te mismatches for strings within a few units of a binary
  midpoint;
- 25-49% directed-mode mismatches for strings near a representable
  value.

**Impact.**

- Exact decimal expansions (`%.60g`) and long literals from other tools
  parse to a different value than strtod's, or with different flags.
- This is a deliberate design decision, but the public header never
  states the two-step procedure.
- Whether every consequence (exact inputs reported inexact; missed
  overflow) fits §5.12.2's allowance should be confirmed against the
  standard's text, which this audit did not have.

**Recommendation.** Document the beyond-H procedure in the header. To
remove the edge entirely, decide beyond-H inputs exactly: compare the
full digit string against the decision midpoint, which needs about
4,000 bits for the longest binary64 expansions (the engine has 3,072).

#### L4. The exponent clamp combined with very long digit strings gives silent wrong answers [V]

**Where.**

- The clamp: `parse_exponent` clamps the exponent magnitude at 100,001
  (`src/msf_string.c:381-401`).
- Digit counts are then applied unclamped: decimal at `:562`/`:566`,
  hex at `:748`.

**What.**

- `"1"` followed by 150,000 zeros and `"e-150000"`, which is exactly
  1.0, parses to **+inf with overflow+inexact** [V].
- Hex analogues fail the same way. `"0x1"` followed by 30,000 zeros
  and `"p-120000"` gives +inf.
- The same input with 100,000 zeros and `e-100000` is correct, so the
  cliff is exactly the clamp.
- No vector can catch it: the generator mirrors the clamp
  (`tests/gen_vectors_str.py:285-286,374-375`), and the vector format
  cannot carry inputs that long.
- Memory safety is unaffected.

**Recommendation.** Saturate the *combined* exponent (exponent field
minus fraction digits plus dropped digits) instead of clamping the
field alone, or clamp after combining.

#### L9. `clz32` in `src/msf_math.c` is off by one, and `big_top_bit` returns a bit length [V]

### 4.4 Info

- **I1. `setPayload(−0)`.** The library treats −0 as the valid payload
  0 and returns `7ff8000000000000`. glibc treats a set sign bit as
  "not a valid payload" and returns +0. IEEE 754-2019 §9.7 speaks of
  a non-negative integer, and −0 is arguably one. This is a reading
  choice; documenting it would remove the ambiguity. Every other
  payload-operation result matched glibc on 12 M checks.
- **I2. Plant keys.** These are answer keys for planted defects, not
  credentials. Twenty-four are in the bundle. Their SHA-256 values
  equal `key_sha256` in `plants.jsonl` for 24/24 [A*]. The
  hash-precommitment rule is visible in history from `c558fb5`
  onwards.
- **I3. Clean room.** No fingerprints of Berkeley SoftFloat, libgcc
  soft-fp, fdlibm, musl, CRlibm/CORE-MATH or dtoa/Ryu were found in
  `src/`, `include/` or `compat/` [A*]. The only shared vocabulary is
  generic: "jam", "Ziv stages".
- **I4. Secrets.** No secrets or credentials were found anywhere in the
  tree or the full 185-commit history. That covers private keys, cloud
  and GitHub tokens, generic key/secret/password assignments and
  high-entropy strings [A*].
- **I5. Personal and host information is published throughout the
  tree.** Presumably the owner's choice, but listed for awareness [A*]:
  - the owner's e-mail on every commit;
  - the Windows username in about 100 files, mostly in the audit
    bundle;
  - the desktop's machine name (AGENTS.md:52, docs/BOARD.md:30,
    docs/HARNESS.md:27);
  - the sandbox account name;
  - the unique IDs of six RP2040 boards;
  - COM port numbers;
  - the Epidemic Sound subscription and the unlisted YouTube link.
  
  History-only revisions carry the same strings, so a HEAD-only
  cleanup would not remove them.
- **I6. Co-author identity.** All 185 commits carry
  `Co-Authored-By: Muse Code (muse-wonder-full-1.0) <noreply@meta.ai>`.
  RECORD.md:28 calls the address "coined by analogy, owner to correct".
  All commits are unsigned.
- **I7. Version predicates.** `is754version1985`/`2008` return 0 and
  `2019` returns 1, which is reasonable. Six of the nine per-format
  version predicates have no vector anywhere; all nine return the right
  values when called [A*].
- **I8. Thin rows.** The thinnest committed-vector rows are the 189
  comparison rows (23-45 vectors each), the 240 to-integer rows (23-33
  each), and nextUp/nextDown/logB (32 each). The nextUp(−minSubnormal)
  corner is hit by exactly one vector per format. The lead's oracle
  covers all of these at volume.
- **I9. Unwired generators.** Generators exist for f16 exhaustive
  rounding/nextUp/logB/conversions/sqrt/toint/totalOrder, xf
  exhaustive, seeded sweeps, string bulk, payload-f16-exhaustive and
  minmax-f16-sweep, but no budget runs them. An agent ran them all:
  14,520,119 vectors, all PASS, in about 3.5 minutes [A*]. Wiring them
  into a weekly CI job would cost little.
- **I10. scaleB rulings.** The second operand is a binary64 encoding.
  The project's choices are internally consistent across all formats
  [A]:
  - `n = ±inf`, or `|n| ≥ 2^13`, saturates the exponent. This gives
    ±inf with overflow+inexact, or ±0/min-subnormal with
    underflow+inexact.
  - Non-integral `n` truncates toward zero.
  - `|n| < 1`, `±0` and subnormal `n` return `x` unchanged.
  
  Clause 5.3.3 expects integral `N`, so this is implementation-defined
  territory. Stating the rulings in the header would help users.
- **I11. Exported internal symbols.** A shared build exports 865
  symbols for 849 declared functions. The extras are `msf_u128_*`,
  `msf_pack_*` and `MSF_FMT_PARAMS` [A*]. They are prefixed, so
  collisions are unlikely; `static` or a visibility attribute would
  make the boundary explicit.

#### I12. API notes for character conversion users [A, spot-checked]

- **Grammar.** No leading whitespace is skipped (`" 1"` gives +0 with
  consumed 0). Hex input requires a `p` exponent (`"0x1.8"` gives +0
  with consumed 0). `"infin"` gives inf with consumed 3. The grammar
  lives only in a source comment (`src/msf_string.c:1-30`); publishing
  it in the header would help.
- **NaN payloads are not carried through text.** Every quiet NaN prints
  `nan`, and `snan` parses back with payload 1. Payload suffixes are
  optional in the standard, so this is allowed, but SF-0 §2.6's
  wording about "payload suffixes this implementation emits" suggests
  more.
- **On a buffer that is too small, nothing is written, not even
  `buf[0]=0`.** The would-be length is returned; this follows the
  documented contract. Callers that ignore the return value see stale
  memory.
- **Digits beyond H on output are zero-padded**, not true digits:
  0.1 at precision 25 prints `1.000000000000000055500000e-01`. INEXACT
  is still correct.
- **`flags == NULL`** crashes only on the paths that raise a flag. The
  header requires non-null.
- **Side note.** The agent's glibc comparison found **glibc 2.39**
  misrounding hexadecimal input with subnormal results: 21 cases in
  about 6 M. For example, `strtof("0X.5B10F0cP-125")` returns `005b10f0`;
  the exact value is 5,968,112.75 smallest-subnormal units, so RNE
  gives `005b10f1`. The library returns `005b10f1` [V]. This is an
  upstream issue, not a project one.

Within H, the character conversions were found **correct in every
direction, format and attribute**:

| Comparison | Volume | Mismatches |
|---|---|---|
| to-decimal vs glibc `%.*e`, plus an exact digit oracle for ties-to-away | about 36 M calls | 0 |
| from-decimal vs `strtod`/`strtof` | about 19 M calls | 0 |
| from-decimal vs exact Fractions | about 1.2 M calls | 0 |
| hex input and output vs glibc and exact oracles | about 20 M calls | 0 |

Sanitizer, valgrind and fuzz runs found no memory error. That covers
size 0/1, exact-fit and NULL buffers, 5,000,000-digit inputs and
3,000,000-digit outputs [A*].

### 4.5 Tier 2 (Clause 9) operations

**Lead evidence [V].** An MPFR 4.2 differential (`mpt2`) emulated
binary32 and binary64 exactly, including subnormals. Flags were derived
independently: inexact from the ternary value, overflow from MPFR, and
underflow as tiny-after-rounding AND inexact. It covered sinPi, cosPi,
exp2, exp2m1, log2, log2p1, rSqrt and hypot in te/tz/tp/tn, with inputs
biased to the hard regions:

- near integers and half-integers, and tiny or large arguments, for the
  trig pair;
- near 1 for log2;
- tiny arguments for exp2m1 and log2p1;
- the overflow and underflow thresholds for exp2.

Result: **8,401,260 checks, 0 mismatches.** Special operands (NaN, ±inf,
±0, poles, domain errors) were excluded here; the project decides them
by table.

**How the code argues correct rounding.**

- **rSqrt and hypot are decided exactly.** `src/msf_alg.c` and
  `docs/WIDTHS.md` describe an exact integer decision:
  - rSqrt: a bit search with the exact compare `Q²·M′` vs `2^(2S)`;
  - hypot: an exact anchored window sum plus an integer root with sticky.
  
  Correct rounding in every attribute follows by construction. This
  is the strongest kind of argument available.
- **sinPi, cosPi, exp2, exp2m1, log2 and log2p1 use Ziv's strategy at
  three fixed precisions** (160, 256 and 384 bits). Error bounds
  `Ek` are proven by generator; a stage's result is accepted when
  both interval endpoints round to the same bits and flags.
  - If the third stage still disagrees, the code silently packs the low
    endpoint (`src/msf_math.c:685`, `:1192`, `:1672`). The undecided
    counter exists only in test builds.
  - "Unreachable" rests on the "leg (c)" margin argument (SF-1 §8,
    SF-2 §4). Stage 3 resolves about 317-318 bits beyond the
    destination precision. The published hardest-to-round binary64
    cases, re-decided with MPFR during the project, need 55.8-69.8
    extra bits; exp2's own maximum is 60.54. That is a margin of about
    5×.
- **What the argument depends on.** The argument is sound in structure.
  But it is only as complete as the external worst-case searches it
  relies on, and those (CORE-MATH-class `.wc` data, used in the
  project's scratch tree) are not in the tree.
- **binary32** additionally rests on the recorded one-time exhaustive
  runs (42,949,672,960 mode-checks each for the exp2 and log2 pairs).
  Their shard logs are not in the tree.
- **binary16** is covered exhaustively by committed vectors.
- **No result in this audit hit a disagreement.** The `-ta-relabel`
  trig vectors were checked separately and are sound (§5).

**Recommendation.**

- Expose the stage-3 fallback in production builds (an assertion hook
  or a readable counter) so that an unreachable branch is observable
  if it is ever reached.
- Record the worst-case sources the argument uses (files, versions,
  SHA-256) in `docs/metrics/fetches.jsonl`.

**Measured kernel error against the claimed bounds.** The worst
relative error, sampled against MPFR, is 2^-156 to 2^-158 at 160 bits,
2^-252 to 2^-254 at 256 bits, and 2^-380 to 2^-382 at 384 bits. The
claimed `Ek` is 146/242/370 (147/243/371 for the exp shapes). The
bounds therefore hold with a margin of at least 9.2 bits. They are
nearly tight for expm1 before the generator's 9-bit reserve.

**Further observations.**

- **The `Ek` proofs assume more than the code does.** The `Ek`
  derivations in `tools/gen_trig_tables.py:109-151` and
  `tools/gen_explog_tables.py:116-154` assume normalized operands with
  the top bit set. Because of the `clz32` convention (L9),
  `wf_normalize` actually produces top-bit-clear significands. Two
  comments are therefore false: `src/msf_math.c:15-17` says "top bit
  of M set", and `:464-469` says "top 128 bits" (really 127).
  - The log2 generator accounts for this (`gen_explog_tables.py:183-190`);
    the trig and exp generators do not.
  - The measured margins above show that the bounds still hold. The
    written proof does not literally match the code.
- **A latent ordering hazard.** `wf_cmp` compares exponents first,
  across the two normalization conventions. No kernel operand pair
  reaches the misordering case.
- **The stage-3 path is described too strongly.** It "silently packs
  the low endpoint", yet RECORD.md:876-877 calls it "an exact
  final-stage decision".
- **Special cells all match the clause readings.**
  - sinPi(±0) = ±0; sinPi at an integer gives a zero with the sign of
    x; cosPi(n+½) = +0 in all five modes.
  - log2(±0) = −inf with divideByZero; log2p1(−1) = −inf with
    divideByZero.
  - rSqrt(±0) = ±inf with divideByZero. MPFR returns +inf for −0; the
    project table-decides this cell to the clause's paired sign.
  - hypot(±inf, qNaN) = +inf.
  - exp2m1 at emax+1 in RZ/RD gives max-finite with inexact only. That
    is correct under Clause 7.4, because overflow is judged on the
    unbounded-exponent result.
- **Augmented operations.** The P1 = R2 underflow reading matches the
  code. That reading cannot be checked here against the standard's
  text.

**Verdict on correct rounding.**

| Format | Status |
|---|---|
| binary16 | covered exhaustively by tests |
| binary32 | the project's one-time exhaustive runs plus tens of millions of independent checks here |
| binary64 sinPi/cosPi/exp2/exp2m1/log2/log2p1 | **argued and tested, not proven** |
| rSqrt, hypot | proven by construction |

The binary64 argument has two parts: a bounded evaluation error with a
large surplus (about 93 extra bits at stage 0, against 56-61 bits of
published worst-case need), and the completeness of external
hardest-to-round lists that are not in the tree. The surplus is what
makes the claim robust in practice; the gate is not (M9).
