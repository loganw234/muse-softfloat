### Special-value tables (Clauses 6 and 7; NaN delivery is always the msf_nan_delivery chain: first signaling operand quieted plus invalid, else first quiet operand, else default quiet NaN plus invalid when invalid_without_nan; FMA order multiplicand, multiplier, addend)

Common: any signaling NaN input signals invalid and delivers that operand quieted (payload converted per the header) for every op below except totalOrder/Mag (never signal) and comparisons (predicate from unordered, invalid still signaled). Quiet NaN inputs deliver the first quiet NaN with no flag for result-bearing ops except as noted for FMA, comparisons signaling family, and to-int.

add/sub (effective signs after sub flips b): inf with finite gives that inf, no flag; inf plus inf same effective sign gives that inf; inf magnitude subtraction (different effective signs) is invalid_without_nan. Finite zeros: same-sign zeros keep that sign; x plus (minus x) exact zero is +0 except roundTowardNegative gives -0 (Clause 6.3); a zero plus a nonzero finite rounds that nonzero once into f32 (cross-format sources mean this is a real rounding, not a copy).
mul: sign is xor. 0 times inf either way is invalid_without_nan. inf times nonzero (finite or inf) gives inf xor-sign, no flag. zero times finite gives zero xor-sign.
div: sign xor. 0/0 and inf/inf are invalid_without_nan. inf over finite (including zero) gives inf, no divisionByZero. finite over inf gives zero. finite nonzero over zero gives inf plus divisionByZero (Clause 7.3). zero over finite nonzero gives zero.
sqrt: negative finite nonzero and -inf are invalid_without_nan (not set for NaN classes, whose sign bit is not a numeric sign). sqrt(+inf)=+inf. sqrt(-0)=-0, sqrt(+0)=+0. Positive finite results are positive (Clause 6.3).
fma: caller computes invalid_without_nan as (0 times inf product) or (inf product magnitude-subtracting an opposite-sign inf addend). If the product is 0 times inf and the addend is a quiet NaN, the caller raises invalid and the helper delivers the addend (SF-0 2.5 ruling). Inf product with finite or same-sign inf addend gives inf of product sign; finite product with inf addend gives that inf. Exact-zero product-plus-addend follows the addition zero rules over (product sign, addend sign); a nonzero exact sum that rounds to zero keeps the exact sign via the pack.
remainder (f32,f32): invalid only for x infinite or y zero (plus NaN rules via the helper with invalid_without_nan set for those). remainder(finite x, inf y) returns x bits exactly; whether the subnormal-x case carries any flag will be read off the Berkeley f32_rem vectors before this path is frozen (Clause 6.1 mentions underflow there, Clause 7.5 says exact underflow raises no flag; the executable authority decides and any split will be escalated, not averaged). Exact zero result takes the sign of x.
roundToIntegral named forms: no flag at all except signaling NaN invalid; the Exact form (attribute rounding) adds inexact exactly when a nonzero fraction was discarded. Zero and inf keep their sign; a magnitude that rounds to zero keeps the operand sign (Clause 6.3, sign of roundToIntegral results is the operand sign).
nextUp/nextDown: NaN via helper (only flag is signaling invalid). nextUp(+inf)=+inf, nextUp(-inf)=-maxFinite, nextUp of either zero is the smallest positive subnormal, nextUp(-minSubnormal)=-0, nextUp(maxFinite)=+inf; nextDown is the mirror (-nextUp(-x)).
scaleB (x f32, N f64): NaN rules over (x, N) in that order first. scaleB(signed 0, N)=that zero, scaleB(signed inf, N)=that inf, scaleB(x, signed 0) returns x. Non-integral finite N truncates toward zero (brief ruling under 5.3.3 language-defined space). Infinite N is treated as a saturating huge exponent of its sign (assumption A1 below); finite huge |N| clamps internally at 10000 in magnitude, far beyond f32 saturation, with overflow/underflow/inexact coming out of the single pack.
logB (source f32, result f64): NaN via helper with dest f64. logB(signed 0) = -inf f64 plus divisionByZero. logB(signed inf) = +inf. Finite nonzero gives floor(log2|x|) as an exact f64 integer (+0 when the exponent is 0); subnormals count leading zeros; the operand sign does not enter except through magnitude.
convertFormat (from f32, from f64): NaN via helper, signed inf/zero preserved, finite single pack (same-format included, which is exact but still quiets a signaling NaN with invalid).
from-int (8): exact magnitude (unsigned negation for the most negative signed values) into one pack; only overflow/inexact/underflow from the pack can arise (large u64/i64 magnitudes round, never overflow f32 range).
to-int (80): NaN of either kind signals invalid and delivers 0. Infinite and rounded-out-of-range signal invalid and saturate (positive to dest max, negative to dest min, unsigned negative to 0) per the header project rule. Inexact is signaled only by the Exact variants, and only when the rounded integer is in range and a nonzero fraction was discarded (assumption A2 below, from the 5.8 wording that the differing result is representable). Range is tested after rounding, so -0.4 toward zero to an unsigned dest is 0 with no invalid, while -0.6 ties-to-even is -1, out of range, invalid to 0. A result of exactly -2^(w-1) for a signed dest is in range; +2^(w-1) is not.
comparisons (44): relation from exact cross-format comparison extended over infinities (same-sign infs equal, signed zeros equal); any NaN makes unordered. Invalid is the only possible flag: always on a signaling NaN; on a quiet NaN only for the compareSignaling family. The 22 predicates are masks over {less, equal, greater, unordered} per Tables 5.1 and 5.2 (12 quiet-family spellings including Unordered/Ordered, 10 signaling-family).
totalOrder/totalOrderMag (5.10, 5.7.2): no flags argument, never signal. Key over binary32 encodings: key = sign set ? bitwise-not(bits) : bits with the sign bit set; totalOrder(a,b) is key(a) at most key(b). This yields -qNaN below -sNaN below -inf below negative finite below -0 below +0 below positive finite below +inf below +sNaN below +qNaN, with NaN payload order the raw trailing-field order inside each class (implementation-defined by 5.10 beyond sign and signaling/quiet placement). totalOrderMag is totalOrder(abs(a), abs(b)) by definition in 5.7.2.

### Per-op algorithm instances of the mandates

Shared: decoded finite operands are sig times 2^exp with sig at most 53 bits (f64 sources). The word-level type scan is respected: the translation unit names no hardware arithmetic type anywhere, comments included.

add/sub kernel (also the back half of fma): split zero cases first (rules above). For two nonzero finite magnitudes, normalize each sig left so its top bit sits at bit 126 of the u128 (exact), tracking the adjusted exponent; jam-shift the lower-top operand right to the dominant exponent, then add or subtract magnitudes by effective signs and pack once with sticky 0 (the jam bit lives inside the sig). Why this is exact enough: when the tops are equal the adjusted exponents are equal, so alignment is exact and deep cancellation is computed exactly; when tops differ, magnitude subtraction can cancel at most to just below the dominant top, so the jam bit at bit 0 stays more than 100 bits below the f32 rounding position and can only act as sticky evidence, which is the WIDTHS.md jam argument. Equal magnitudes with opposite signs produce the Clause 6.3 exact-zero sign directly, never through the pack.
mul: msf_u128_mul_u64_u64 over the two sigs (at most 53x53, exact in u128), exponents add, one pack.
div: normalize dividend and divisor sigs to 64-bit tops A and B (exact left shifts). The long division is of A shifted left 63 (under 2^127, inside u128) by B, done as a 128-step shift-and-subtract over u128 remainders, producing a 63-64 bit quotient Q and remainder R. Exact quotient value is (Q + f) times 2^E with f = R/B in [0,1) and E collecting ea-eb, the normalization length difference, and -63; the pack receives Q with sticky = (R != 0). That is p+2 bits many times over for p=24, with exact remainder evidence.
sqrt: make the exponent even (sig left 1 if needed), then scale the sig left by an even shift s so the scaled integer fills about 126 bits. Integer root by bit construction over 64 candidate bits, each trial squared exactly with msf_u128_mul_u64_u64 and kept iff its square does not exceed the scaled sig. Sticky is (scaled sig - Q^2 != 0); pack at exponent ea/2 - s/2 with sign 0.
fma: exact product as in mul (48 bits for f32xf32, up to 106 for f64xf64) with product sign xor; if the addend is zero, pack the product (zero product plus zero addend goes through the zero rules); if the product is zero, the back half is the add kernel over (zero of product sign, addend); otherwise feed (product sig, product exp) and (addend sig, addend exp) into the same normalize-to-bit-126 jam-align add/subtract-magnitude machinery and pack once. The top-126 normalization is what lets a 106-bit product and a 53-bit addend share one u128 window with the same exactness argument as add (equal tops align exactly; unequal tops keep the jam bit far below the rounding position).
remainder: work with magnitudes X, Y and signs sx, sy on the f32-only domain (sigs at most 24 bits). Compute floor quotient parity and R0 = X - floor(X/Y)*Y by feeding the dividend bit stream (significand bits followed by exponent-difference zeros, at most about 300 steps) through a shift-and-subtract divider whose remainder stays under 2Y; the exponent-difference-negative case uses the mirror stream against the shifted divisor in u128, with a trivial path when 2X is certainly below Y. Then compare 2*R0 with Y: below keeps R0 with sign of x; above takes Y-R0 with the opposite sign; exactly equal is the tie, resolved by floor-quotient parity (even keeps R0). The chosen remainder is packed once at its exact scale (it is representable; tie and zero signs per the table).
roundToIntegral: exponent at least 0 means already integral, return the operand bits. Otherwise split sig at shift -exp into integer part and fraction, compare the fraction with the half bit pattern, increment the magnitude per the named mode (or the attribute for the Exact form), and pack the integer at exponent 0 through a scratch flags word so no inexact or underflow can leak from the pack; the Exact form then adds inexact itself iff the fraction was nonzero.
nextUp/nextDown: unsigned encoding step on the magnitude bits (positive: up is +1, negative: up is -1, zeros special-cased to the smallest subnormal of the requested direction); maxFinite steps to inf encoding naturally.
scaleB: decode N as f64; finite N truncates toward zero to an integer in the significand domain (negative-exponent shifts drop the fraction, huge exponents clamp to +-10000); pack x sig at exp plus that integer once. N of either zero sign returns x bits for finite x.
logB: integer exponent from the decoded top bit position (bit length of sig plus exp minus 1, exact for normals and subnormals alike), delivered through the f64 pack at exponent 0 (exact small integers, sign from the exponent value). This is the one place a binary64 pack is used; the destination really is logBFormat.
convertFormat and from-int: direct single packs as tabled.
to-int: round in the magnitude domain exactly as roundToIntegral but with the unclamped integer kept in u128 (f32 magnitudes reach under 2^128 when the exponent is nonnegative), decide inexact-for-Exact from the discarded fraction, range-check the signed result against the destination width in u128 (negative handled against 2^(w-1)), saturate and flag invalid outside, otherwise deliver the low bits in the C integer type.
comparisons: one static helper computes the relation (msf_cmp_finite for finite pairs, explicit infinity rules otherwise) and the invalid rule; 44 thin wrappers pass a relation mask and the signaling-family bit.
totalOrder/Mag: key function above.
