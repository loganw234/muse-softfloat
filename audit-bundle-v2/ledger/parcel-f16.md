### Special-value tables (Clauses 6 and 7; NaN delivery is always the msf_nan_delivery chain of SF-0 2.5: first signaling operand quieted plus invalid, else first quiet operand, else default quiet NaN plus invalid when invalid_without_nan; FMA order multiplicand, multiplier, addend; payloads shift-align into binary16, so an f32/f64 NaN whose payload truncates to zero delivers the binary16 default quiet NaN 0x7e00 with no extra invalid for the truncation itself)

Common: any signaling NaN input signals invalid and delivers that operand quieted (payload converted per the header) for every op below except totalOrder/Mag (never signal, no flags argument) and comparisons (predicate from unordered, invalid still signaled). Quiet NaN inputs deliver the first quiet NaN with no flag for result-bearing ops except as noted for FMA, the comparison signaling family, and to-int. Binary16 constants: max finite 0x7bff (65504), smallest normal 0x0400, smallest subnormal 0x0001, infinities 0x7c00/0xfc00, default quiet NaN 0x7e00.

add/sub (effective sign after sub flips b): inf with finite gives that inf, no flag; inf plus inf same effective sign gives that inf; inf magnitude subtraction is invalid_without_nan. Finite zeros: same-sign zeros keep that sign; x plus (minus x) exact zero is +0 except roundTowardNegative gives -0 (Clause 6.3); a zero plus a nonzero finite packs that nonzero once into f16 (f32/f64 sources make this a real rounding, not a copy, and it can overflow to inf or round to zero per the mode).
mul: sign is xor. 0 times inf either way is invalid_without_nan. inf times nonzero (finite or inf) gives xor-signed inf, no flag. zero times finite gives xor-signed zero.
div: sign xor. 0/0 and inf/inf are invalid_without_nan. inf over finite (including zero) gives inf, no divisionByZero. finite over inf gives zero. finite nonzero over zero gives inf plus divisionByZero (Clause 7.3). zero over finite nonzero gives zero.
sqrt: negative finite nonzero (normal or subnormal) and -inf are invalid_without_nan (never for NaN classes, whose sign bit is not a numeric sign). sqrt(+inf)=+inf. sqrt(-0)=-0, sqrt(+0)=+0. Finite results are positive (Clause 6.3).
fma: caller computes invalid_without_nan as (0 times inf product) or (infinite product magnitude-subtracting an opposite-sign inf addend). If the product is 0 times inf and the addend is a quiet NaN, the caller raises invalid and the helper delivers the addend (SF-0 2.5 ruling, mirrored from msf32.c). Infinite product with finite or same-sign inf addend gives inf of the product sign; finite product with inf addend gives that inf. Exact-zero product-plus-addend follows the addition zero rules over (product sign, addend sign); a nonzero exact sum that rounds to zero keeps the exact sign via the pack.
remainder (f16,f16): invalid only for x infinite or y=0 (plus NaN rules via the helper with invalid_without_nan set). remainder(finite x, inf y) and remainder(signed 0, finite nonzero y) return x bits exactly, no flag. Nonzero result sign comes from the defining relation r = x - n*y with n the ties-to-even integer (so remainder(5,3) = -1); an exact-zero result takes the sign of x. The result is exact, so no inexact/underflow is expected; my exact reference asserts exactness case by case and any Berkeley split is an escalation, not an average.
roundToIntegral named forms: no flag at all except signaling-NaN invalid; the Exact (attribute) form adds inexact exactly when a nonzero fraction was discarded. Zero and inf keep their sign (operand bits returned); a nonzero magnitude that rounds to zero keeps the operand sign (Clause 6.3). Operands with decoded exp at least 0 are already integral and return unchanged.
nextUp/nextDown: NaN via helper (the only possible flag is signaling invalid). nextUp(+inf)=+inf, nextUp(-inf)=-0x7bff, nextUp of either zero is +0x0001, nextUp(-0x0001)=-0, nextUp(0x7bff)=+inf; nextDown is the mirror.
scaleB (x f16, N f64): NaN rules over (x, N) in that order first, delivered as f16. scaleB(signed 0,N) and scaleB(signed inf,N) return x unchanged; N of either zero sign returns x. Finite non-integral N truncates toward zero (brief ruling in the 5.3.3 language-defined space; |N| below 1 therefore acts as 0). Infinite N acts as a saturating exponent of its sign (lead-confirmed ruling A1 of 2026-10-26 21:55, adopted unchanged), and finite huge |N| clamps internally at 10000, far beyond binary16 saturation, with overflow/underflow/inexact coming out of the single pack.
logB (source f16, result binary64 logBFormat): NaN via helper with dest f64. logB(signed 0) = -inf as f64 plus divisionByZero. logB(signed inf) = +inf. Finite nonzero gives floor(log2|x|) as an exact f64 integer (+0 when that exponent is 0); subnormals count their leading zeros through the decoded top-bit position; the operand sign does not enter except through magnitude.
convertFormat (from f16, f32, f64): NaN via helper, signed inf/zero preserved, finite single pack. Same-format from_f16 is exact on finite values but still quiets a signaling NaN with invalid.
from-int (8): exact magnitude (unsigned negation for the most negative signed values) into one pack. There are no NaN/inf inputs; above 65504 the pack delivers overflow per the mode (inf or max finite) with overflow+inexact, and in-range inexact magnitudes signal inexact from the pack only.
to-int (80, source f16): NaN of either kind signals invalid and delivers 0. Infinite and rounded-out-of-range signal invalid and saturate per the public header rule (positive to dest max, negative to dest min, unsigned negative to 0). Inexact is signaled only by the Exact variants, and only when the rounded integer is in range and differs from the operand (lead-confirmed ruling A2 of 2026-10-26 21:55, adopted unchanged). Range is tested after rounding: -0.4 toward zero to an unsigned dest is 0 with no invalid, while -0.6 ties-to-even rounds to -1, is out of range, and delivers 0 with invalid. Exactly -2^(w-1) is in range for a signed dest; +2^(w-1) is not. Finite f16 magnitudes are below 2^17, so i64/u64 saturation is reachable only through inf/NaN, while i8/u8/i16/u16 saturate on ordinary finite values.
comparisons (66): relation from the exact cross-format comparison extended over infinities (same-sign infs equal, signed zeros equal); any NaN makes unordered. Invalid is the only possible flag: always on a signaling NaN, and on a quiet NaN only for the compareSignaling family (Clause 5.11). The 22 predicates are masks over {less, equal, greater, unordered} per Tables 5.1/5.2 (14 quiet-family spellings including Unordered/Ordered, 8 signaling-family orderings plus signaling equal/not-equal naming in the generated header - implemented exactly per the COVERAGE list: quiet_equal, quiet_not_equal, signaling_equal, signaling_not_equal, quiet_greater, quiet_greater_equal, quiet_less, quiet_less_equal, quiet_not_greater, quiet_not_less, quiet_less_unordered, quiet_greater_unordered, quiet_unordered, quiet_ordered, signaling_greater, signaling_greater_equal, signaling_less, signaling_less_equal, signaling_not_greater, signaling_not_less, signaling_less_unordered, signaling_greater_unordered), each at (f16,f16), (f16,f32), (f16,f64).
totalOrder/totalOrderMag (5.10, 5.7.2): no flags, never signal. Key over binary16 encodings: key = sign set ? bitwise-not(bits) : bits with the sign bit set; totalOrder(a,b) is key(a) at most key(b). This yields -qNaN below -sNaN below -inf below negative finite below -0 below +0 below positive finite below +inf below +sNaN below +qNaN, and inside the negative NaN classes a larger payload gives a smaller key, which is the payload-descending order the brief names from the Wave 1 verifier note N1. totalOrderMag is totalOrder(abs(a),abs(b)).

### Per-op algorithm instances of the mandates (mirror of src/msf32.c throughout; deviations named)

Shared: decoded finite operands are sig times 2^exp with sig at most 11 bits (f16), 24 (f32), 53 (f64). Every result-bearing op ends in exactly one msf_pack_f16 call (logB alone packs f64, same recorded exception as msf32_log_b). The translation unit names no hardware arithmetic type anywhere, comments included, so the word-level type scan stays clean.

add/sub kernel (also the back half of fma), mirrored from msf32.c add_mag: zero cases split first (Clause 6.3 rules above; one-side-zero packs the other operand once). For two nonzero finite magnitudes, normalize each sig left so its top bit sits at bit 126 of the u128 (exact), tracking the adjusted exponent; jam-shift the lower adjusted-exponent operand right to the dominant exponent, then add or subtract magnitudes by effective signs and pack once with sticky 0 (the jam bit lives inside the sig). Invariant, restated where the kernel lives: every sig reaching this alignment has bit length at most 106 (decoded source at most 53, exact product at most 106), so after normalization it carries at least 21 trailing zeros; when the tops are equal the adjusted exponents are equal and alignment is exact, so deep cancellation is computed exactly, and when they differ, magnitude subtraction can cancel at most to just below the dominant top, so the jam bit at bit 0 stays more than 100 bits below the binary16 (p+2 = 13) rounding position and can only act as sticky evidence. Equal magnitudes with opposite signs produce the Clause 6.3 exact-zero sign directly, never through the pack.
mul: msf_u128_mul_u64_u64 over the two sigs (11x11 up to 53x53, exact in u128), exponents add, one pack.
div, mirrored: normalize dividend and divisor sigs to 64-bit tops A and B (exact left shifts), long-divide A shifted left 63 by B with a 128-step shift-and-subtract over u128, producing quotient Q (62 to 64 bits) and remainder R. The exact quotient is (Q + f) times 2^E with f = R/B in [0,1) and E collecting ea-eb, the normalization length difference, and -63; the pack receives Q with sticky = (R != 0). That is p+2 bits many times over for p=11, with exact remainder evidence, and Q is never zero, so the pack domain (no zero sig with sticky) is respected.
sqrt, mirrored: make the exponent even (sig left 1 if needed), scale the sig left by an even shift so the scaled integer fills about 126 bits, integer root by bit construction over 64 candidate bits with each trial squared exactly by msf_u128_mul_u64_u64, sticky = (scaled sig - Q^2 != 0), pack at exponent ea/2 - shift/2 with sign 0.
fma, mirrored: exact product as in mul (22 bits for f16xf16 up to 106 for f64xf64) with xor product sign; zero product feeds add_mag as an exact zero of the product sign so the zero rules see (product sign, addend sign); otherwise (product sig, product exp) and (addend sig, addend exp) go through the same normalize-to-bit-126 jam-align machinery and one pack. No intermediate rounding exists because no pack runs before add_mag.
remainder, mirrored with one width change: magnitudes X, Y as the decoded sigs (at most 11 bits), signs sx, sy. For ea >= eb, stream the 64-bit container bits of X followed by (ea-eb) zeros (at most 29) through a shift-and-subtract divider against Y in uint64, keeping only floor-quotient parity (the last quotient bit) and R0 = X - floor(X/Y)*Y in units of 2^eb. For ea < eb with s = eb-ea, return x bits at once when s >= 12, because then B*2^s >= 4096 > 2*2047 >= 2X so 2X is certainly below Y and n = 0; this threshold is p+1 at p=11 and replaces the binary32 parcel's 25 (deviation, forced by width). Otherwise mirror-stream X against Y shifted left s in u128 (s at most 29 fits trivially), giving R0 in units of 2^ea. Then compare 2*R0 with the divisor in the same units: below keeps R0 with the sign of x; above takes the complement with the opposite sign; exactly equal is the tie, resolved by floor-quotient parity (even keeps R0). An exact-zero remainder is delivered as a signed zero of x's sign directly; otherwise one pack at the exact scale, which the reference asserts is exact case by case.
roundToIntegral: decoded exp at least 0, zero, or inf returns the operand bits. Otherwise split sig at shift -exp into integer part and fraction, compare the fraction against the half pattern, increment the magnitude per the named mode (or the attribute for the Exact form), and pack the integer at exponent 0 through a scratch flags word so no inexact or underflow can leak from the pack; the Exact form then adds inexact itself iff the fraction was nonzero. Only values below 1024 in magnitude reach the split, so the integer is always exactly representable.
nextUp/nextDown: unsigned encoding step on the magnitude bits (positive: up is +1, negative: up is -1, zeros special-cased to the smallest subnormal of the requested direction); 0x7bff steps to the inf encoding naturally, derived as MSF_F16_INFINITY-1 rather than a literal table.
scaleB, mirrored including its clamp shape: decode N as f64; a top-bit exponent of 14 or more clamps to plus/minus 10000 (binary16 saturates long before that, and the pack turns the clamped exponent into the correct overflow/underflow delivery and flags); a top below 0 truncates to 0 and returns x; otherwise truncate N toward zero in the significand domain and pack x sig at exp plus that integer once.
logB, mirrored: integer exponent from the decoded top-bit position (bit length of sig plus exp minus 1, exact for normals and subnormals alike), delivered through the f64 pack at exponent 0.
convertFormat and from-int: direct single packs as tabled.
to-int, mirrored: round in the magnitude domain with the same splitter as roundToIntegral, decide Exact-inexact from the discarded fraction, range-check the rounded magnitude against the destination width (negative against 2^(w-1) for signed, any nonzero negative out of range for unsigned), saturate with invalid outside, otherwise deliver the low bits in the C integer type. At f16 widths the unclamped integer is below 2^17, so no u128 headroom question arises.
comparisons: one static helper computes the relation (msf_cmp_finite for finite pairs, explicit infinity rules otherwise) and the invalid rule; 66 thin wrappers pass a relation mask and the signaling-family bit across the three source pairs.
totalOrder/Mag: the 16-bit key above, totalOrderMag on sign-cleared operands.
